The Project

About This Guide

The Concept

A video documentary series filmed across thirty countries on six continents. In each location, the same dish is prepared: Ragù alla Bolognese, following the official recipe deposited by the Accademia Italiana della Cucina at the Bologna Chamber of Commerce on 20 April 2023. The canonical recipe is the reference point. Everything else is a variation — and a question.

The One Rule

Every chapter contains one locally-sourced, culturally specific ingredient not in the canonical recipe. It must integrate into the cooking process — not be used as garnish. It must change when or how something is cooked, not simply what is served alongside. It must be defensible on flavour grounds without breaking the dish's essential identity.

Two Worlds

Part One covers fifteen European countries — geographically close to Bologna, culinarily in conversation with it. Part Two goes further: fifteen countries across Asia, the Americas, Africa, the Middle East, and Oceania. The further the dish travels, the more it reveals about what ragù actually is at its core, stripped of its geography.

Adaptations Noted

In some countries, the canonical fresh pork pancetta is not culturally or legally available. Where this occurs — Morocco, Turkey, Nigeria — the adaptation is noted explicitly and the substitution (lamb belly fat, beef suet) is argued on flavour grounds. These moments are part of the story. In India, water buffalo mince is used in states where beef is restricted; beef where freely available.

The Foundation

The Canonical Recipe

Ingredients — Serves 6
Coarsely ground beef (cartella / pancia / fesone)400g
Fresh pork pancetta — not smoked, not cured150g
Strained tomatoes (passata)200g
Double-concentrate tomato paste1 tbsp
Onion (peeled)~60g
Carrot (peeled)~60g
Celery stalk (trimmed)~60g
Red or white wine (dry)~100ml
Whole milk (optional)~100ml
Extra virgin olive oil3 tbsp
Light broth or stock cubeas needed
Salt and pepperto taste
✓ Permitted (2023)

White or red wine. Olive oil or butter (50g).

✗ Not Permitted

Cream. Smoked pancetta. Garlic. Herbs.

The 2023 recipe's key change: tomato reduced from 300g to ~250g effective while beef increased from 300g to 400g. Ratio shifted from 1:1 to ~1:2 (tomato:meat). The statement: a meat sauce with tomato — not a tomato sauce with meat. Every recipe in this guide respects that as its floor.

Method
1
Finely dice pancetta; render slowly in a heavy 24–26cm pan with EVOO over medium-low heat until fat runs and pieces are pale gold.
2
Add finely chopped onion, carrot, and celery. Sweat gently until soft — 8–10 minutes without browning.
3
Add ground beef. Stir over raised heat until the meat sizzles and sputters — the Bolognese "sfrigola."
4
Pour in wine. Stir gently until fully evaporated — 3–4 minutes.
5
Add strained tomatoes and tomato paste. Cover partially and simmer on lowest heat for a minimum of 2 hours, adding warm broth ladle by ladle as needed.
6
Toward the end, stir in whole milk to temper the acidity of the tomato. Adjust salt and pepper.
7
Serve with fresh egg tagliatelle. Never spaghetti.

Going Further

Fifteen countries beyond Europe. The further the dish travels from Bologna, the more it reveals about what ragù actually is.

Asia · The Americas · Africa · The Middle East · Oceania
New currencies. New fats. New ferments. Same two-hour simmer.

Two notes before you proceed. In Morocco and Turkey, fresh pork pancetta is replaced with lamb belly fat — the honest adaptation for predominantly Muslim food cultures, and one that produces a genuinely superior result in each context. In Nigeria, the deglazing liquid is palm wine, not grape wine. In India, water buffalo mince is used where beef is restricted by law.
